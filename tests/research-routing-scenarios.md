# Research closeout routing scenarios

Use the actual closeout references and `knowledge-integrity.md` when reviewing
these cases. Give only the task/state column to an independent reader, then
compare its routing decisions with the expected boundary. These are decision
scenarios, not a measurement of research quality or sustained agent behavior.

| Case | Task and research state | Expected routing and boundary |
|---|---|---|
| Mixed conclusions | A reproducible experiment supports a bounded workload limit and leaves a broader reliability hypothesis unresolved. | Preserve the useful packet; promote the supported durable limit to one owner with its scope and evidence; retain the unresolved hypothesis with confidence, missing evidence, and recheck trigger. These actions can share a batch. |
| Several supported claims | One packet supports a product acceptance decision and a distinct benchmark selection rule. | Route each supported conclusion to its own unique owner; preserve shared evidence once and link it. Do not copy the entire packet into both owners or infer delivery from the accepted decision. |
| All unresolved | Initial observations are useful, but no conclusion yet supports an authoritative current fact. | Keep useful evidence and explicitly uncertain claims in the packet; do not force promotion, declare verified, or discard solely because the question remains open. |
| Negative finding | A rejected approach failed under a documented setup and would otherwise be tried again. | Preserve the reproducible negative finding and its limits. Promote a lasting decision or warning only when the evidence and authority support that conclusion. |
| Existing packet | New measurements extend the same research question, while some claims were already promoted. | Update the existing packet, retain prior provenance, and update only affected current owners. Do not create a redundant packet or re-promote unchanged claims merely to show activity. |
| Invalidation | New evidence undermines a claim already promoted into architecture. | Record the new evidence and update the unique architecture owner through the confidence/conflict rules; updating the packet alone must not leave an invalid current claim standing. |
| No reusable material | A disposable run adds no useful observation or claim and the existing owner already preserves its information. | Record a concrete exclusion reason; do not create an empty packet or treat exclusion as authorization to delete source files. |
| One-off observation | A local test records one successful result, with no broader durability or reliability evidence. | Retain only the bounded observation when useful. Do not promote it into a universal product promise or a verified production guarantee. |

Packet preservation, evidence confidence, adoption, and current implementation
are separate decisions. Passing a routing scenario does not prove the promoted
claim itself; that still requires fitting evidence and the applicable review.

boundaries. It preserved combined packet/claim handling, unresolved and negative
findings, unique current owners, invalidation updates, source-file authorization,
and limits on one-run evidence. It also found an old exclusive-route shorthand
in the routing appendix and a missing benchmark owner in the confidence

measurement of full research workflows or sustained consumer behavior.
